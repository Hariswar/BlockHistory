### Revised plan (Carondelet)
- **2026-10-10 — Study area moves from The Hill to Carondelet, St. Louis.** The Hill only has 1903 maps online; Carondelet (Vol. 12) has 1938 and 1938–1950 scanned, which covers the car era, when gas stations, garages and dry cleaners appear. It also has a heavy industrial riverfront next to homes. *(Supersedes the Hill entries above. The Hill's config and 5 labeled sheets stay as a pre-car map style for evals.)*
- **2026-10-10 — The 1916 Vol. 12 doesn't match Carondelet; 1908 Vol. 8 does.** Volume numbers moved between editions. In 1916, Vol. 12 covered Dutchtown/Gravois (only sheets 47–50 touch Carondelet's west edge). The 1908 key map of Vol. 8 shows riverfront Carondelet (Bates to the River Des Peres, sheets ~47–106), and every 1938 Vol. 12 sheet names its 1908 Vol. 8 equivalents. So the third map year is 1908 Vol. 8, not 1916 (Phase 3).
- **2026-10-10 — The Carondelet config keeps all 74 Vol. 12 sheets, tagged by area.** 33 Carondelet, 14 Patch, 27 west of Grand (Holly Hills, Boulevard Heights, parks). The whole volume is only 155 images, inside the budget; tagging lets Carondelet and Patch go first. The 1950 scan has no sheet 11.
- **2026-10-10 — Annotation sample is riverfront and Broadway (sheets 50, 53, 57, 62, 74).** These cover the main contamination types (coke/gas plant, foundries, bulk oil, filling stations, dye works) plus one mostly residential sheet (62) to test false positives.
- **2026-10-10 — Free reader routing idea from Day 7: Tesseract finds names, not symbols.** On 1938 tiles Tesseract got 16 of 24 hand labels but 0 of the G.T. gas-tank symbols. Any tile where Tesseract finds FILL'G, GARAGE, AUTO, OIL or BULK, or that has small circles near the curb, should go to the vision model for tank counting. (Week 4–5.)
- **2026-10-12 — Qwen2.5-VL 7B (default settings) is not good enough as the free first pass.** On 3 sheet-50 tiles it found 3 of 10 hand labels (Tesseract: 6 of 10), missed every filling station and the bulk oil station, looped on repeated entries until the 4,096-token cap, and took ~150 s per tile. Before dropping it, try in week 4: a short names-and-G.T.-only prompt, a repeat penalty, 512 px crops, or the 32B model on Kaggle. Fallback: Haiku on every tile fits the budget (~$2.60 for both editions). (Revisit after the week 7 comparison.)
- **2026-10-12 — Claude Haiku 5.5 becomes the main map reader; the free vision model is dropped for now.** On the same 3 tiles, Haiku (low effort) found 9 of 10 hand labels and all 3 gas-tank sites for $0.0035 total, against 3 of 10 and no tanks for Qwen2.5-VL 7B and 6 of 10 for Tesseract. At ~$0.001/tile, both Carondelet editions cost ~$0.86 with the Batch API, under 10% of the $10 budget, so "free first" isn't worth the accuracy loss. Tesseract stays as a free cross-check on printed names. *(Supersedes the free-first routing in the plan's tech stack.)* (Revisit if per-tile cost rises above ~$0.003 or a free model clears 8 of 10 on these tiles.)
- **2026-10-12 — Prompt v1, low effort; streets come from georeferencing, not the model.** Prompt v1 added abbreviation rules (FR. = frame, BR. = brick, FOUND. = foundation), tank-placement rules, "block numbers aren't addresses", and skipping plain D/S/F/A footprints. On the 3 test tiles it removed the extra (misplaced) gas tank and the misread abbreviations, and cut output about a third; labels stayed 9/10 and tank sites 2/3. Medium effort scored worse (8/10, 1/3) at almost twice the cost, so the default for map reading is **low**. Street names stayed unreliable (side-street names on S. Broadway numbers), so the reader keeps the printed house number and the street is assigned in weeks 8–9 from each building's georeferenced position. (Revisit if tank-site recall stays below ~80% on the week 7 eval.)
- **2026-10-12 — Gas tanks are a count, not yes/no.** `gasoline_tank_count` in the reader's schema matches the hand labels, and scoring only credits a tank when it's on the right building.
- **2026-10-12 — All tiles go through the Batch API; no blank-tile filter.** Carondelet's sheets are dense: only 13 of 1,500 tiles in the 1938 edition are nearly blank, so filtering would save under 1%. Batch reads cost ~$0.0004 per tile (prompt v1, low effort), so both editions (~2,980 tiles) should cost ~$1.20, well under the plan's estimate.
- **2026-10-12 — Spot-check lists are committed; merged results are not.** `evals/spotcheck/*.md` hold your ticks (human judgment, can't be regenerated), so they go in git. `data/results/` and the caches are reproducible from the cache and stay out of git. Reports never overwrite a checklist that has ticks.
- **2026-10-13 — How accuracy is scored.** A finding matches a hand-labeled site by label words, one finding per site, with street numbers deciding between same-named sites (a one-digit difference counts as a misread, not a different site). Precision counts findings rated high or medium; a named part of a labeled plant (CUPOLA, SECTION 1) counts as correct, and duplicate reads are reported on their own. Matching uses the printed label, not the reader's description, so calling "FR. FOUND." a foundry never scores. Human-verified and draft labels are reported apart.
- **2026-10-13 — Claude may draft labels, but only checked files count as ground truth.** Sheets 47, 49, 56, 61 and 67 were drafted from the images to reach week 6 faster; they stay `draft` until checked by eye. The headline baseline uses only the 5 human-verified sheets.
- **2026-10-13 — Merge only across a shared tile edge, never within a tile.** A building in a corner overlap shows on all four tiles, so a diagonal-only pair is two sites. Two entries from one tile are two buildings. This fixed the duplicate numbered/unnumbered reads without merging sheet 47's two filling stations.
- **2026-10-13 — Baseline: names are good enough, gas-tank counts are not.** On the 5 checked sheets, recall 83% and precision 88%, but tanks landed on only 6 of 10 tank sites and 12 of 25 tanks were read. That is under the ~80% tank-site line from Oct 12, so before reading the whole volume, test a tank-focused second pass on tiles that have a filling station, oil or garage finding (e.g. a short "count the G.T. circles at this site" prompt, or medium effort on just those tiles). Cost check first: about 1 tile in 6 would qualify. (Revisit once the label drafts are checked.)
- **2026-10-13 — Add a full-resolution gas-tank pass instead of a bigger model or higher effort.** The first pass found the stations but missed their circles, which are ~6 px wide on 50% tiles; medium effort had scored worse on Day 7. Re-reading only fuel/vehicle tiles at full resolution with a tanks-only question raised tank sites from 6/10 to 9/10 and exact counts from 4/10 to 7/10 on the checked sheets, for $0.038 on 10 sheets. Rules that came from the first run: tanks with no named site go to the tile's only fuel/vehicle finding (or are dropped), counts take the max over overlapping crops instead of adding, and a group naming only "D." never creates a site. Tank-site recall is now above the 80% line. (Revisit if misattributed tanks keep showing up: next idea is to list the tile's first-pass sites in the prompt so each group is given to one of them.)
- **2026-10-13 — Georeference with street intersections from OpenStreetMap and an affine fit, no GIS libraries yet.** One Sanborn sheet spans under 2 km, so a flat local projection and 6 parameters are enough: leave-one-out error is 1–3 m on sheets 47 and 56. Pure Python keeps the install light; GDAL/QGIS are only needed if a sheet bends enough to want a polynomial fit. Control points are QGIS `.points` files so they can be checked or fixed by hand. (Revisit if a sheet's median error is over ~8 m.)
- **2026-10-13 — Locate findings with a point pass, not the reader's position words.** Position words were off by 27–55 m on sheet 56; asking Haiku for each flagged building's pixel center (a list of that tile's labels plus the image) got 13 of 15 within 4 m, for ~$0.0001 per tile. Only high/medium findings and tank sites get it; low findings keep the rough position. Accuracy is measured against positions measured on the map, because the Census geocoder was 46–84 m off on known buildings.

Each panel is its own row in the sheets table, so the Supabase schema didn't change.

Claude's checks there don't change a listing's "checked by a person" status.
